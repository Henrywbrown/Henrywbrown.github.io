Restorations & Hobby Work

Below are a few of my main restoration projects as well as a number of smaller hand tool restorations. Restoration is something that comes to me very naturally, it allows me to express creativity while using tools to collect more tools. I began restoring hand tools at the passing of my Grandfather, as he had left for us a large amount of old tools. Some restorations are meant to only achieve a usable state while other restorative work is aimed for perfection, my work falls somewhere in between.

Rockwell Delta 70-400

I purchased a Rockwell Delta 20" drill press in very rough condition (pictured adjacent). The press weighs approx. 500 lbs and was previously toppled over causing extensive damage to the motor and tensioner mechanism, as well as the table lift rack and pinion. I bought the drill press for scrap price and salvaged the intact worm gear from table lift to repair my Clausing 22S drill press.

Rockwell Delta 70-400 Restoration

In order to restore the 70-400 I swapped the inopperable 3-phase motor for a single phase motor. To do this I turned a brass output shaft adaptor on my Clausing 5914 lathe to make the 1/2" shaft of the motor fit the 3/4" sheave on the press. I also turned a new quill lock screw. The biggest issue was the broken table lift. I bought a trailer jack, cut it appart, and fabricated it to bolt on the table and clamp around the drill post. It allowes the table function properly.

Clausing Machines

Pictured adjacent is my Clausing 5914 36" lathe and my Clausing 22S 20" drill press. I bought the two machines separately in non-working condidition. I have spent a lot of time cleaning, repairing, and fine-tuning the two machines. As they stand, the two machines are both usable and have increased the capabilities of my shop greatly.

Blacksmith Projects

I have very briefly dabbled in the art of smithing. The projects I made were made in a homemade charcoal forge and were shaped on a railroad iron anvil. The projects were all successfully quenched, hardened, and annealed.

The "216"

Before my brother and I constructed our utility vehicle, we resurrected our father's John Deere 216 garden tractor. It was going to be hauled off to scrap because it sat for 22 years, was crushed by a dead tree, and did not run. Despite these issues my brother and I got it running like a top, rebuilt its broken pieces and even added a pusher blade we made from an old water tank to the front.
