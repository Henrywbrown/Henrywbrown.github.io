About Me

I am a third year student at Wisconsin Lutheran College in Milwaukee, Wisconsin majoring in physics with a math minor. I work as team lead of the Student Life Office, and serve on the Student Conduct Board at the college, in addition I am a TA helping to teach Physics at Wisconsin Lutheran High School down the street. My hometown is Houston, Texas where I have spent countless hours working on projects in my workshop. My love for mechanical work stems from my father who for 17 years worked as a generator technician. Weather it is working on family vehicles in the backyard or completing service projects for my Church community my love for building and fixing things is only as great as my passion for serving others through my work.

My Approach

A great literature teacher once taught me: "All good writing is re-writing." The same applies to design; it's rare that a first design is perfect. Real engineering lies in identifying failure. Once the time for redesign comes, it's important to treat the sickness and not the symptom. I see failure as a tool, and it's important to have lots of tools when building or fixing.

My Experience

Aside from being a full-time shade tree mechanic for my family's fleet of high-mileage vehicles, I have spent the last three summers working 50 hrs a week as a CNC machinist. My time at H&W Manufacturing has taught me a number of things on top of how to run a machine. The first is that for as much as I know there is twice as much to learn. The second is that assumption is the mother of all mistakes. The third, but certainly not final, is how to pay close attention even when unwarranted. Some claim that CNC machining completely eliminates the connection between the operator and the machine. Having run both manual and CNC I can confidently say this is untrue. Every vibration in the floor, noise behind an interlock, chip around a tool, and cloud of coolant smoke is the machine talking, and you have to be attuned to listen.

My Goals

I may not be studying engineering as an undergrad; however, I hope that my strong physics and math education along with my experiences can land me an internship. My current plan is to pursue a masters in an engineering discipline after I graduate in May 2028. Until then, I will continue my research at WLC while collecting more tools and learning new skills.
